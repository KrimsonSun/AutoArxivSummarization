\begin{abstract}
We evaluate a 3-LLM debate pipeline with retrieval-augmented verification
(\textsc{Ours}) against five single-LLM baselines on summarising 29 arXiv
papers across four sub-fields (NLP, vision, RL, diffusion). Our pipeline
runs three heterogeneous open-weights models (Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen-2.5-72B,
DeepSeek-V3) in parallel as draft writers, then orchestrates a verifier,
paragraph-level retriever, and refiner stage. To control evaluation bias
we score every summary under two independent regimes — a strict
LLM-as-judge with FActScore-style atomic-claim recall, and a local
DeBERTa-v3-NLI classifier that is fully decoupled from the generation
stack. Both evaluators agree on the same ranking: \textsc{Ours} attains
the \emph{lowest} F1 across all six methods (0.37 strict-LLM, 0.33 DeBERTa),
while a single-call Qwen-72B baseline tops both (0.57 / 0.50), driven by
\textsc{Ours} only covering 24\% of the paper's atomic facts versus 42\%
for the strongest baseline. Diagnostic analysis identifies four mechanisms
behind the regression: (i) the refiner's ``merge-strongest'' prompt biases
toward intersection rather than union of facts, (ii) the verifier's
\texttt{missing\_info} type only fires when \emph{all} drafts miss a fact,
(iii) the pipeline never sees the paper's atomic-fact inventory, and
(iv) the structured output schema rewards abstraction over specificity.
We outline coverage-aware fixes that re-orient debate toward fact union.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multi-agent LLM systems\,---\,where several language-model instances
exchange drafts, critiques, and revisions\,---\,have been promoted as a
general recipe for improving factuality and reasoning beyond
single-call inference~\cite{du2023improving,wu2023autogen,liang2023encouraging}.
Recent work on iterative self-refinement augmented with retrieval (\textsc{iLLMV}
\citep{illmv2025bigdata} and related) has further suggested that grounding
the debate in document evidence should mitigate the hallucinations that
plague free-form summarisation.

We set out to test whether a state-of-the-art instantiation of this idea
\,---\,three heterogeneous 70B-class open-weights models in parallel,
followed by a verifier-driven retriever and refiner\,---\,actually
delivers higher factual coverage than the simplest possible baseline:
a single LLM call with a one-line prompt. Our null hypothesis was
that the debate pipeline would dominate. Our finding is that
\emph{it does not}: across 29 arXiv papers the multi-agent system
achieves the \emph{lowest} F1 under two independent evaluation regimes,
covering only 24\% of the paper's atomic facts where the best
single-call baseline covers 42\%.

This paper contributes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
    \item A complete open implementation of a 3-LLM debate +
          verifier + paragraph-level RAG + refiner pipeline,
          \textsc{RefinedSummarization}, plus a faithful evaluator that
          implements both LLM-as-judge and DeBERTa-NLI variants.
    \item A reproducible 29-paper benchmark (seed-fixed sampling
          from a curated pool spanning NLP, vision, RL, diffusion)
          comparing \textsc{Ours} against five single-LLM baselines
          (Llama-70B, Qwen-72B, DeepSeek-V3, Llama-8B, Llama-70B with a
          structured prompt).
    \item A negative result with cross-validation: under both an
          LLM-as-judge regime and an entirely decoupled
          DeBERTa-v3-NLI~\cite{he2021debertav3} regime, \textsc{Ours}
          ranks last on F1 (0.37 / 0.33) versus the best baseline (0.57 /
          0.50). The two judges disagree on absolute precision values
          but agree on the ranking, ruling out the dominant
          confound for LLM-as-judge.
    \item A diagnosis of four mechanisms responsible for the regression
          and a sketch of coverage-aware fixes that re-orient the debate
          toward fact \emph{union} rather than \emph{intersection}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Multi-agent LLM systems.}
\citet{du2023improving} introduce multi-agent debate, in which two or
more language models iteratively critique each other's answers, and
report gains on arithmetic and factual question answering. AutoGen
\citep{wu2023autogen}, MetaGPT \citep{hong2023metagpt}, and CAMEL
\citep{li2023camel} extend this to role-played collaboration. The
common premise is that diverse agents collectively cover blind spots
that any individual agent misses. Our results question whether this
premise holds when the task is long-form factual summarisation rather
than short-answer reasoning.

\paragraph{Self-refinement and verification.}
Self-Refine~\cite{madaan2023selfrefine} and Chain-of-Verification
\citep{dhuliawala2023chainofverification} demonstrate that prompting an
LLM to critique and revise its own draft can substantially reduce
hallucinations. \textsc{iLLMV}~\cite{illmv2025bigdata} adapts these
ideas with Jaccard-style self-consistency for legal element extraction
and is the most direct ancestor of our pipeline.

\paragraph{Factuality evaluation.}
\textsc{FActScore}~\cite{min2023factscore} formalises atomic-claim
factuality: extract atomic facts from a generated summary, and verify
each against the source. SummaC~\cite{laban2022summac} pioneers
NLI-based verification using a classifier rather than the generation
LLM as judge. We adopt both perspectives, complementing summary-side
precision with paper-side recall, and contrast LLM-as-judge against
DeBERTa-v3-NLI to detect potential bias.

\paragraph{Retrieval-augmented generation.}
RAG~\cite{lewis2020retrieval} grounds generation in retrieved evidence;
DPR~\cite{karpukhin2020dpr} introduces dense retrieval. We use BM25
\citep{robertson1995okapi} for paragraph retrieval, both within our
pipeline and inside the evaluator.

\section{The \textsc{RefinedSummarization} Pipeline}
\label{sec:method}

\textsc{Ours} is a 3-LLM debate pipeline with retrieval-augmented
verification and a single refiner stage. The full architecture is shown
in the leftmost column of Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline_compare}; here we
describe the key choices.

\paragraph{Three heterogeneous draft writers.}
We instantiate three drafters from independent organisations:
\textsc{Llama-3.3-70B} (Meta), \textsc{Qwen-2.5-72B} (Alibaba), and
\textsc{DeepSeek-V3} (DeepSeek). All three are open-weights and routed
through OpenRouter. Each receives the parsed paper paragraphs and is
asked to produce a structured summary with fields
\{\texttt{tldr}, \texttt{core\_idea}, \texttt{key\_contributions},
\texttt{method}, \texttt{experiments}, \texttt{limitations}\}, with
explicit \texttt{evidence\_refs} pointing back to paragraph IDs.

\paragraph{Verifier.}
The three drafts are passed to a single verifier (Llama-3.3-70B with a
strict prompt) that returns a list of \emph{issues} typed as
\texttt{factual\_error}, \texttt{missing\_info}, \texttt{inconsistency},
\texttt{unsupported\_claim}, or \texttt{ambiguity}. Crucially the
\texttt{missing\_info} type fires only when \emph{all three drafts}
omit a fact present in the paper.

\paragraph{Paragraph-level retriever.}
For each issue, a BM25 retriever fetches the top-5 most relevant paper
paragraphs as evidence. Retrieved IDs are echoed verbatim from the parsed
paper to prevent the retriever LLM from hallucinating new content.

\paragraph{Refiner.}
A second Llama-3.3-70B call ingests the three drafts, the issue list, and
the per-issue evidence bundles, and produces the final
\texttt{FinalSummary}. The refiner prompt directs the model to
\textit{``merge the strongest content from all three drafts''} and to
correct factual errors using the cited evidence. As we show in
Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis}, this prompting choice biases the merge
operation toward \emph{intersection} of facts rather than union.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:eval}

We evaluate every summary under three increasingly strict regimes,
which differ only in their verifier (Step~3 in
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline_compare}).

\paragraph{Common pipeline (Steps 1, 2, 5, 6).}
For every (summary, paper) pair: (1) an LLM extracts atomic claims
from the summary; (2) BM25 retrieves the top-5 paper paragraphs per
claim; (5) once per paper, an LLM extracts up to 40 atomic claims from
the paper itself, cached and reused across all six methods so every
method is scored against the same denominator; (6) an LLM judges, for
each paper claim, whether it is covered by the summary, in
\{\texttt{Covered}, \texttt{Partial}, \texttt{NotCovered}\}.

\paragraph{Loose mode.}
The Step-3 verifier uses a lenient prompt and steps 5--6 are skipped.
This is the simplest version of FActScore-style summary-side precision.

\paragraph{Strict-LLM mode.}
The Step-3 verifier uses a tightened prompt requiring numeric and
named-entity matches; vague claims downgrade to \texttt{Partial}.
Steps 5--6 are enabled, yielding paper-side recall and F1.

\paragraph{DeBERTa-NLI mode.}
Step~3 is replaced by \textsc{DeBERTa-v3-base-mnli-fever-anli}
\citep{he2021debertav3}, an 86M-parameter classifier fine-tuned on
$\sim$800k human-labelled NLI examples. For each (claim, paragraph) pair
we forward-pass and take the maximum entailment / contradiction
probability across the top-5 retrieved paragraphs, with thresholds
$0.70/0.30/0.50$ for the four-way verdict. The classifier was never
trained on outputs from any of the generation models in our experiments,
so any agreement with strict-LLM rules out same-model self-favouring.

\paragraph{Headline metrics.}
\begin{align*}
\text{Precision} &= \tfrac{|S| + 0.5\,|P|}{|S|+|P|+|U|+|C|}\\
\text{Recall}    &= \tfrac{|\text{Cov}| + 0.5\,|\text{Par}|}{|\text{Cov}|+|\text{Par}|+|\text{NC}|}\\
\text{F1}        &= \tfrac{2\,\text{P}\,\text{R}}{\text{P}+\text{R}}\\
\text{Hal.}      &= \tfrac{|U| + |C|}{|S|+|P|+|U|+|C|}
\end{align*}
where $|S|, |P|, |U|, |C|$ denote summary-side counts of
\{Supported, Partial, Unsupported, Contradicted\}, and
$|\text{Cov}|, |\text{Par}|, |\text{NC}|$ denote paper-side
\{Covered, Partial, NotCovered\}.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/pipeline_compare.png}
    \caption{Three evaluation pipelines applied to every (summary, paper)
    pair. Steps shaded with red borders differ across modes; all other
    steps are identical. Loose mode produces only summary-side precision
    (saturated and uninformative in our experiments). Strict-LLM and
    DeBERTa-NLI both compute paper-side recall and F1, but use different
    Step-3 verifiers (LLM-as-judge versus a local NLI classifier) and

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Paper sample.}
We curate a pool of 30 well-known arXiv papers spanning NLP architecture
(Transformer, BERT, GPT-3, PaLM, Mistral, BitNet), parameter-efficient
adaptation (LoRA, Prompt Tuning), alignment (InstructGPT, Constitutional
AI, DPO), reasoning and agents (CoT, ReAct, ToT, AutoGen), vision and
multimodal (ViT, CLIP, DALL-E 2, SAM), diffusion (DDPM, Stable
Diffusion), and retrieval (RAG, DPR). One paper (\texttt{2106.04561},
Linformer) failed PDF parsing and was dropped, leaving 29 papers. The
sample is fixed by \texttt{random.seed(42)}.

\paragraph{Methods.}
We compare six methods. \textsc{Ours} is the full pipeline of
Section~\ref{sec:method}. Five single-LLM baselines isolate different
aspects:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=1pt,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textsc{B1 Llama-70B-naive} --- one Llama-3.3-70B call,
    \textit{``Summarize this paper.''}
    \item \textsc{B2 Qwen-72B-naive} --- one Qwen-2.5-72B call, same prompt.
    \item \textsc{B3 DeepSeek-V3-naive} --- one DeepSeek-V3 call, same prompt.
    \item \textsc{B4 Llama-8B-naive} --- one Llama-3.1-8B call as a
    cost / quality lower bound.
    \item \textsc{B5 Llama-70B-structured} --- one Llama-3.3-70B call
    with a sectioned prompt requesting TL;DR, contributions, method,
    experiments, and limitations. This isolates ``architecture''
    from ``prompt engineering''.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Cost.}
The full experiment (29 papers $\times$ 6 methods $\times$ 3 evaluation
modes $=$ 522 \texttt{EvaluationReport} JSON files) consumed
approximately \$12 of OpenRouter credit and ran in $\sim$3~hours of
wall-clock time with paper-level concurrency of~8. All weights, configs,
and seed-frozen sampling guarantee bit-for-bit reproducibility on the
deterministic stages and identical paper / claim sets across modes.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Loose mode is saturated}

Under loose evaluation, all six methods score in the
\textbf{0.91--0.98} range for evidence coverage and \textbf{0.01--0.06}
for hallucination. The metric is unable to distinguish a 14-claim
naive summary from a 22-claim structured one, in line with the well-known
brittleness of summary-side precision when the denominator is the
summary itself. We treat loose-mode results as a sanity baseline and
focus the analysis on the two strict regimes.

\subsection{Strict-LLM and DeBERTa agree on the ranking}

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/results_table.png}
    \caption{Mean across 29 papers under all three evaluation modes.
    Yellow cells highlight F1, the head\-line strict-mode metric.
    Higher F1 and lower hallucination are better.
    \textsc{Ours} attains the lowest strict-LLM F1 (0.37) and the lowest
    DeBERTa-NLI F1 (0.33) across all six methods; the strongest baseline
    is \textsc{B2 Qwen-72B-naive} under both modes (0.57 / 0.50).}
    \label{fig:results_table}
\end{figure*}

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.49\textwidth]{figures/mean_strict.png}
    \hfill
    \includegraphics[width=0.49\textwidth]{figures/mean_deberta.png}
    \caption{Per-method mean precision, paper recall, F1, and
    hallucination across 29 papers. Left: strict-LLM. Right: DeBERTa-NLI.
    Error bars are population standard deviations. The two evaluators
    disagree on absolute precision (LLM-judge gives systematically higher
    precision than DeBERTa, consistent with same-model self-favouring),
    but agree on the F1 \emph{ranking}: \textsc{Ours} is last and
    \textsc{B2 Qwen} is first under both regimes.}
    \label{fig:means}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:results_table} and Figure~\ref{fig:means} report the
mean values across the 29 papers. Two findings stand out.

\paragraph{(1) Multi-agent debate is the worst method.}
Under strict-LLM, \textsc{Ours} achieves
$F1 = 0.37$, the lowest of the six methods, while
\textsc{B2 Qwen-72B-naive} attains $F1 = 0.57$, a 0.20-point absolute
gap. The pattern repeats under DeBERTa-NLI:
$0.33$ for \textsc{Ours} versus $0.50$ for the same Qwen baseline. The
second strongest method under both regimes is also a single-call
baseline (\textsc{B3 DeepSeek-V3-naive}, F1 0.53 / 0.46).

\paragraph{(2) The two evaluators rule out LLM-judge bias.}
The strict-LLM evaluator (Llama-3.3-70B) is the same model that
participates in our pipeline, so a raw concern is that it might
preferentially reward its own conditional outputs. The DeBERTa-NLI
evaluator was never trained on any output from Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen-2.5,
or DeepSeek-V3, and yet produces an identical ranking. The two
evaluators \emph{do} disagree on absolute precision values
(LLM-judge: 0.91--0.97 across methods, DeBERTa: 0.60--0.70), consistent
with strict-LLM being more permissive on vague claims, but the F1
ordering is invariant.

\subsection{The regression is driven by recall, not precision}

\textsc{Ours} attains the highest \emph{precision} under DeBERTa
(0.68, tied) and a competitive 0.96 under strict-LLM. The deficit is
entirely on recall: \textsc{Ours} covers only 0.24 of the paper's atomic
facts (strict-LLM and DeBERTa agree to two decimals), versus 0.42 for
the strongest baseline. The summary length is comparable (mean
21 vs.\ 23 atomic claims), so \textsc{Ours} is not simply ``writing
less''\,---\,it is writing the wrong things. The same atomic facts are
covered in our drafts but \emph{lost in the merge}.

\subsection{Robustness}

We pre-registered an n=7 pilot with seed-frozen sampling and ran the
full n=29 confirmation later. The relative ranking and the gap to
\textsc{Ours} are stable: F1 of \textsc{Ours} moves from 0.28 to
0.37 (strict-LLM, n=7 vs.\ n=29) but remains in last place; the gap to
the best baseline is 0.21 at n=7 and 0.20 at n=29.

\section{Diagnosis}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

The information loss between draft and final output points to the
refiner stage. We identify four mechanisms.

\paragraph{(M1) Refiner prompt biases toward intersection.}
The refiner is instructed to \textit{``merge the strongest content from
all three drafts.''} When two drafts carry a specific number and the
third does not, the refiner often \emph{drops} the number rather than
preserving it. We observe this on every paper that contains numerical
benchmark results.

\paragraph{(M2) Verifier coverage-gap blindness.}
The verifier's \texttt{missing\_info} issue type fires only when all
three drafts miss a fact present in the paper. Facts present in only one
or two drafts therefore generate no signal to the refiner, even though
they are exactly the cases where multi-agent diversity should pay off.

\paragraph{(M3) No paper-side awareness.}
The pipeline never inspects the paper's atomic-fact inventory: the
verifier and retriever are issue-driven, and the refiner reads only the
drafts and verifier output. There is no mechanism that says
\textit{``the paper claims X; check that it survives the merge.''}

\paragraph{(M4) Structured schema rewards abstraction.}
The output schema (\texttt{tldr / core\_idea / method / experiments /
limitations}) encourages thematic phrasing over specific facts.
Refiners filling these slots from three diverse drafts default to the
most abstract phrasing common to all drafts, again favouring
intersection.

These four mechanisms compound: a coverage-aware verifier alone cannot
help if the refiner still merges by intersection, and an
intersection-resistant refiner alone is uninformed about which paper
facts it should preserve.

\section{Toward a Coverage-Aware Pipeline}
\label{sec:fix}

We outline four targeted fixes that re-orient the debate toward fact
\emph{union}. Their full evaluation is beyond the scope of this paper
and is reported in a companion work.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Union-biased refiner prompt}. Replace
    \textit{``merge the strongest''} with \textit{``preserve every
    specific fact (number, dataset, method name) that appears in any of
    the three drafts; only drop a fact if explicitly contradicted by
    evidence.''} Add a final pass that lists draft-only facts and asks
    the refiner why it included or excluded each.
    \item \textbf{Coverage-gap verifier issue}. Extend the verifier
    schema with a \texttt{coverage\_gap} type that fires when fact $f$
    is supported by paper evidence and present in $\le 2$ of the 3
    drafts. The retriever fetches evidence for $f$ and the refiner
    is required to consider it.
    \item \textbf{Paper-aware Step 0}. Run an atomic-claim extraction
    over the paper before draft generation, and condition all three
    drafters on a ``must-cover checklist'' of $\le 20$ paper facts.
    This trades pure debate for a partial outline but should
    sharply improve recall.
    \item \textbf{Fact-density reward in schema.} Add a per-section
    constraint that each non-trivial sentence must cite at least one
    paragraph ID, raising the cost of abstract phrasing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

(i) $n = 29$ is large enough to invalidate \textsc{Ours} but too small
to support strong claims about the cause.
(ii) Both evaluators rely on an LLM for atomic-claim extraction
(Step 1) and paper-claim extraction (Step 5); this LLM is the same
Llama-3.3-70B used as our pipeline's verifier. Even though the
DeBERTa Step-3 verifier removes self-favouring at the verification
step, claim \emph{selection} could still favour our pipeline's
preferred phrasings.
(iii) DeBERTa-v3-base has a 512-token context, so paragraphs are
truncated when long. We do not observe systematic bias from this.
(iv) Both judges score one (summary, paper) pair at a time and do not
see other summaries; they cannot diagnose ranking-style errors.
(v) We use BM25 retrieval; a dense retriever could change which
evidence is fed to the verifier and thereby shift verdicts.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A 3-LLM debate pipeline grounded in retrieval-augmented verification
underperforms the simplest single-LLM baseline on summarising 29
arXiv papers, with the gap driven by paper-side recall rather than
hallucination. Two independent evaluators\,---\,LLM-as-judge and
DeBERTa-v3-NLI\,---\,agree on the ranking, ruling out the most common
LLM-as-judge confound. We attribute the regression to a refiner
prompted to \emph{merge} rather than \emph{union} drafts, a verifier
blind to single-draft coverage gaps, and the absence of paper-side
awareness in the pipeline. We hope this concrete failure mode informs
the design of future agent pipelines, and we encourage the community
to evaluate multi-agent systems against single-call baselines under
strict, decoupled judges before claiming improvements.
